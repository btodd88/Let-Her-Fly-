A soldier's words

Written somewhere in Iraq.

During Operation Desert Storm, SFC Frazier served as a combat engineer and platoon sergeant. He later served as acting First Sergeant for B Company, 317th Engineer Battalion. In February 1991, amid the sand, machinery, tents and long days of deployment, he wrote a poem about the flag, sacrifice and what it means to carry the colors of home far from home.

Let Her Fly

As written by SFC Frazier • Iraq • February 1991

Hiss and howl ol' desert wind
Upon this endless sea of sand
But blow you lightly upon these colors
For they are set by the blood of man

Whites so bright and pure
Every star and bar a story untold
Of many battles and of wars
Fought with faithful hearts ever bold

Blood red bars
The gauze from their wounds

Spilled on the countless battlefields
America, know these wounds were for you

So deep and true it's blue
It is the souls given
Not for it's symbol
But for which it tolls

Dark blue for the
strength of a nation
And for it's pride too

All you that are American
Can you gaze upon it without tear in eye

I can not
For I know so many have and will die

Blow you over the world wide
Ol' mighty wind
Unfurl “Old Glory” wherever she flies
Straight out
For I have a tear in my eye

LET HER FLY, FOREVER FLY

SFC FRAZIER SOMEWHERE IN IRAQ, FEB “91”
